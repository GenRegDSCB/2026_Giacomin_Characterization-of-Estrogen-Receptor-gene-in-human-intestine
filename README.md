# 2026_Giacomin_Characterization-of-Estrogen-Receptor-gene-in-human-intestine
Characterization of estrogen receptor expression in the human intestine across cell types, sex and age 
Biological sex and gender drive significant molecular dimorphism across human tissues, playing an important role in defining sex-specific gene expression networks that influence physiological processes, disease susceptibility, and responses to therapy. These differences have been observed in different tissues, including the gastrointestinal tract, where sex-based differences can play a crucial role in modulating intestinal physiology, immune responses, interactions with the intestinal microbiota, and even susceptibility to pathological conditions such as colorectal cancer. In investigating sexual dimorphisms, particular interest has focused on the role of estrogens and Estrogen Receptors (ERα and ERβ), nuclear receptors that regulate key biological processes such as cell growth and differentiation. However, despite evidence from animal models about the role of ERs in intestinal tissues, little is known about their activity in humans, which integrative bioinformatic analysis of omics data can now explore.
In this thesis project, through an integrative analysis of bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, we examined ESR1 and ESR2 expression across different regions and cell types of the human intestine. Specifically, we processed data from GTEx and the Gut Cell Atlas using reproducible bioinformatics analyses to characterize gene expression across cell types, sex and age. The analysis showed that both ER-coding genes are highly expressed across intestinal regions, particularly in the ileum and colon, and that expression differs significantly by age and sex. Cell type-specific analysis showed that B cells, particularly plasma cells, of the small and large intestine are the cellular population characterized by the highest levels of ESR1 and ESR2 expression. Analysis of these gene expressions in B cell subclasses highlighted IgA plasma cells as the cell population with the highest levels in both the small and large intestine. Conversely, small intestine memory and naïve  B cells were characterized by low ER expression.
Differential expression analysis of IgA plasma cells stratified by ER expression showed that ER-expressing cells were enriched for genes involved in transcription regulation and immune responses, while pathways associated with oxidative phosphorylation were downregulated. These results demonstrated, for the first time, a candidate ER regulatory activity in intestinal B cells. Understanding the intricate relationship between estrogen signaling and intestinal adaptive immunity, particularly mediated by IgA plasma cells, could provide insight into their role in host-microbiome interactions and in pathological conditions affecting the intestinal tract.
